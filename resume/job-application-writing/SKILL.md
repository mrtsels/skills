# Job Application Writing

Use when the user needs any written piece of a job application: a personalized cover letter, a cold outreach email to a company or hiring manager, or answers for an online application form. All three share one framework — research the role, match the employer's needs, and lead with evidence.

## 1. Prepare Before You Write

1. Read the job posting and list its top 3–5 requirements in priority order.
2. Research the company: product, mission, recent news, and the specific person you are writing to (hiring manager, recruiter, team lead).
3. Pull matching evidence from the resume — one concrete achievement per requirement.
4. Decide the channel and tone: formal letter, short cold email, or form answers, matched to the company culture.

## 2. Cover Letter Structure

1. **Header**: your contact info, date, company info.
2. **Opening**: name the specific role, how you found it, and a hook (1–2 sentences).
3. **Body paragraph 1**: achievement that maps to the top requirement.
4. **Body paragraph 2**: second key achievement showing a different strength.
5. **Body paragraph 3**: cultural fit and motivation for this specific company.
6. **Closing**: call to action plus a confident closing statement.

## 3. Cold Email Structure

1. **Subject line**: specific, personalized, non-spammy.
2. **Opening**: common ground or a genuine compliment (1 sentence).
3. **Value proposition**: what you bring (1–2 sentences).
4. **Specific ask**: meeting, referral, or advice (1 sentence).
5. **Close**: gratitude and an easy opt-out.

## 4. Application Form Responses

1. **Contact info**: copy straight from the resume.
2. **Work history**: format for text fields — company, title, dates, then bullets.
3. **Education**: degree, institution, dates.
4. **Skills**: comma-separated or select from the provided list.
5. **Cover letter / additional info**: a shorter version of the cover letter.
6. **Demographics and equal-opportunity questions**: optional; answer or skip, using standard responses.

## 5. Shared Rules

- Address a specific person when known; otherwise use the hiring team.
- Reference company-specific details (product, mission, news, a post or talk).
- Keep every claim backed by resume evidence; never misrepresent information.
- No generic phrases; match tone to company culture.
- One page max for letters; 5–7 sentences for cold emails.
- One clear call to action per outreach; make it easy to say yes.
- Keep form answers concise — forms have character limits — and use exact dates from the resume.
- Match job titles to what the form expects.
- Save a copy of everything submitted; follow up on cold emails after 5–7 days and track response rates.

## Pitfalls

- Generic opening like "I am writing to apply for..." with no hook.
- Claims in the letter that the resume cannot back up.
- Cover letter longer than one page.
- Cold email with no specific ask or with multiple asks.
- Spammy subject lines or writing before researching the recipient.
- Fabricating or inflating information on application forms.
- Inconsistent dates or job titles between form and resume.
- Forgetting to save a copy of submitted form answers.
