# McKinsey-style headline titles & storyline design

How to title every slide so the deck reads as one argument, per the user's rule:
"麦卡锡风格的一句话headline也是storyline…所有页面的storyline可以单独拿出来组成一段完整的话，页面的内容要为storyline解释。"

## The rule

1. Every page title = ONE complete declarative sentence. Subject + verb + object,
   conclusion first, no framing words ("We will discuss…", "Overview of…").
2. Never a noun phrase or topic label. "Settlement" ✗ — "SR3 settles in cash —
   daily variation margin, then one final payment from the official SOFR Index" ✓.
3. Divider/section pages participate too: the big title is a storyline sentence;
   the chapter name ("Part 02 · The USD Derivatives Toolkit") becomes the subtitle.
4. Cover page keeps its brand title; its subtitle can be the storyline opener.
5. Content evidences the headline. If the headline says "margins are risk-based
   performance bonds", the slide shows HOW (initial vs maintenance, SPAN, waterfall).

## Writing checklist

- Put the LOAD-BEARING claim in the title — a reader who only reads titles gets
  the full argument.
- Keep it under ~95 chars so it wraps to ≤2 lines at 26pt on 13.33in-wide slides
  (title box h≈1.0, valign top; content starts y≥1.6).
- Numbers carry the claim when possible: "After 525bp of hikes and 100bp of cuts,
  rates hold at 3.50–3.75% — and markets now price hikes".
- Use em-dash apposition for the punchline: "… — the FOMC target anchors all USD rates".
- Read the whole title list top-to-bottom before shipping: it must flow as one
  paragraph. Rewrite any title that breaks the flow or repeats the previous one.

## Worked example — 27-title storyline (Task-5 rates deck, Jul 2026)

Cover subtitle: "A storyline in five parts — the benchmarks, the USD toolkit,
the EUR mirror, and a hawkish 6–12M outlook"
Agenda: "The storyline — from two benchmarks to a hawkish 6–12 month trade view"

1. "Two overnight benchmarks — fed funds and SOFR — anchor every USD rate"
2. "Fed funds is small, unsecured, and policy-critical — the FOMC target anchors all USD rates"
3. "SOFR's $3T/day secured repo market replaced LIBOR and is now the benchmark of choice"
4. "EFFR and SOFR track each other nearly tick-for-tick, but differ in credit, volume and role"
5. "After 525bp of hikes and 100bp of cuts, rates hold at 3.50–3.75% — and markets now price hikes"
6. "SR3 futures are the pricing engine of the USD rates market"
7. "The USD derivative landscape is broad, but 3-month SOFR futures are its pricing engine"
8. "SR3 is a cash-settled contract that prices one quarter of compounded SOFR at $25 per basis point"
9. "SR3 pricing is linear and transparent: price equals 100 minus the implied rate"
10. "SR3 settles in cash — daily variation margin, then one final payment from the official SOFR Index"
11. "Margins are risk-based performance bonds — roughly $300–600 initial on a $1M notional"
12. "DV01 — $25 per contract per basis point — is the yardstick for sizing, hedging and limits"
13. "Four building-block trades put SR3 to work: direction, curve, hedging and carry"
14. "Europe mirrors the toolkit — EURIBOR and €STR futures share the 100-minus-rate logic"
15. "EURIBOR and €STR differ by a ~27bp credit premium — and both feed deep futures markets"
16. "SR3, F3M and FST3 share one pricing convention but differ in benchmark, venue and basis"
17. "Both curves point one way — rates higher over the next 6–12 months"
18. "The curve has bear-steepened — front end −58bp, long end +30–40bp — as markets price hikes"
19. "US policy is on hold today with hike risk tomorrow: SEP dots, data and pricing all agree"
20. "Trade the hawkish repricing with payers, steepeners and carry — inside a fixed DV01 budget"
21. "The ECB hiked in June and markets price ~1.6 more hikes by year-end — ahead of the Fed"
22. "USD–EUR policy divergence is the trade: express it via F3M/FST3 strips and FX-hedged payers"
23. "Scenario planning makes the base case tradeable and the tails survivable"
24. "Summary — carry plus steepeners under a hawkish base case, with the tails hedged"
25. "Appendix — all data sources, so every claim can be verified"

Note how each title is the argument of its page, the numbers are in the titles,
and the em-dash punchline pattern repeats without feeling formulaic. Divider
titles (6, 14, 17) are slightly shorter sentences that summarize their part.
